THE BIG PICTURE

Protection is growing.
But where does it matter?

Australia is home to diverse landscapes and threatened wildlife. Protected areas are important for conservation, but their coverage differs across states and territories. This story examines the geography of protection, threatened-species observations and changes over time.

Is Australia's protected land growing?

Protection meets biodiversity

Larger circles represent more ALA observations; darker green represents higher protected-land coverage.

Circles are approximately geographically arranged. This is a Dorling-style cartogram prototype.

KEY INSIGHT

Australia's protected-land coverage more than doubled from 10.1% in 2002 to 22.6% in 2024. However, threatened-species observation records remain unevenly distributed, with NSW recording particularly high counts. Expanding protected land does not necessarily mean biodiversity observations are equally distributed across states.

FIGURE 08

Which management categories protect the most land?

This treemap compares the land area protected under different IUCN management categories across Australia. Larger rectangles represent greater protected area.

IUCN Category VI accounts for approximately 57.6% of Australia's terrestrial protected-area coverage, followed by Category II at 22.8%. Source: CAPAD 2024.

KEY INSIGHT

Threatened-species observation records are unevenly distributed across Australia, with noticeable concentrations in eastern and southeastern regions. Meanwhile, IUCN Category VI accounts for approximately 57.6% of Australia's terrestrial protected-area coverage, showing that sustainable-use protected areas make up the largest share of the conservation network.

THE TAKEAWAY

Protecting more land
is only the beginning.

Australia's protected-area network has expanded, but its distribution differs across jurisdictions. Threatened-species observations offer an additional perspective, while highlighting the need for careful interpretation of biodiversity data.
